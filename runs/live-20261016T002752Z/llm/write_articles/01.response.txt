# Your agent got an app. Your gadgets are still catching up.

*This month's biggest change was agents with access to your accounts going mainstream. The hardware built around them is mostly still on paper.*

*As of 2026-10-16 · Edition evidence window: 2026-09-17 to 2026-10-16*

Ethan Mollick says his personal agent did something small and useful this month. Meta's Muse noticed on its own that one of his airline credits was about to expire, and when he asked, it contacted American Airlines to request an extension [01-c3].

That is not a benchmark result or a keynote demo. It is an agent reading someone's accounts, spotting a problem nobody had asked about, and then acting on it. Mollick also says this is no longer a niche experiment. He reports that Muse was the number one app in the App Store at the start of October, and that OpenAI has released a competitor called dots [01-c1].

Here is the argument. The development that mattered most between mid-September and mid-October was neither a gadget nor a smarter model. It was proactive agents with access to your accounts becoming a mass-market consumer product. Glasses, operating systems and home devices are being redesigned to give those agents somewhere to live, but most of that redesign is still promised rather than shipped.

## 1. Personal agents went mass-market

Google moved too. It has merged its Spark agent into the main Gemini chat as a "Task" mode [01-c2]. The fine print matters, though. 9to5Google reports that the change is rolling out server-side on Android and is not yet showing up on iOS or the web, and Google says agent tasks are for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers [01-c2].

The first-hand reports show why this ranks first. On the Vergecast (15:15–16:20), The Verge's Allison Johnson said she expected calling her dots agent to "feel weird and dumb." She then spent about ten minutes going section by section through a website redesign it had drafted, and concluded the call "was better than sitting and typing, you know, paragraphs of thoughts" [01-c4]. She was also candid that the result "wasn't perfect" [01-c4].

There is a caveat. The App Store ranking comes from Mollick, a commentator who is currently promoting a book, and no one in our evidence measured it independently. No source gives usage or retention figures for any of these agents. Topping the charts in launch week is not the same as becoming part of people's routines.

## 2. Glasses met a privacy reckoning

Smart glasses are the most visible personal-context device, and this month the privacy question stopped being hypothetical. Meta released its first camera-free glasses, Ray-Ban Meta Audio, at $349, shipping from October 13 [01-c5]. In the same weeks, Norway's government proposed a temporary ban on smart glasses in selected public places [01-c6]. The ban is not law yet. It still has to be introduced as legislation and approved by Norway's parliament [01-c6].

The evidence here is lopsided. Everything we have on glasses comes from Meta. Other companies' glasses exist in this window only as leaks, which we don't use.

## 3. The operating system is being rebuilt, mostly on paper

Microsoft's own post separates what has shipped from what has been promised. It says its agent containment layer, Microsoft Execution Containers (MXC), is generally available on Windows 11 now. The Copilot features that use local context and take actions on your PC are only "expected to begin rolling out on Copilot+ PCs in the coming months" [01-c7]. So the safety fence exists, but the thing it is meant to fence in does not yet. Nothing has shipped, so nobody has tested it hands-on, and we rank it as a promise.

Voice comes fourth. It is finding narrow, real roles, such as Johnson's phone call, and piece 03 takes that question up.

## Why the agent outranks any device

Taken one at a time, this month's launches look unrelated: a phone app, a pair of glasses, a Windows update, a home device. They look different when read together. MKBHD's review of Apple's HomeView (MKBHD, 04:09) describes a camera that recognises who walks up to it and then answers from that person's messages and calendar. On the Vergecast (57:22), the hosts argued that every company's hardware idea boils down to giving your AI agent a surface on which to act for you. Put together, our reading is that this month's new hardware exists chiefly to give an agent personal context and a place to work [01-c8].

That is an editorial judgment, not a measurement of impact. It also leaves gaps. Users outside the US, people outside tech, and people with accessibility needs show up here only through vendor announcements, never in their own words.

## What to watch

- **Retention, not rankings.** The first credible numbers on how many people still use Muse, dots or Gemini's Task mode a month later would test whether this wave is real.
- **Who gets access.** Watch whether Gemini's agent tasks reach iOS, the web, or anyone without a paid tier.
- **Windows promises turning into product.** Watch whether Copilot's local actions actually roll out, and what independent testers find once they do.
- **Norway's parliament.** If the ban passes, the camera in your glasses becomes a legal question as well as a social one.

If agent usage holds up and the devices follow, this ranking stands. If the apps fade after launch week, the hardware built for them will be left with nothing to carry.
