# Assistants That Hold Your Keys: What Actually Changed in AI Interfaces This Month

*As of 2026-10-15*

## Scene 1: An agent that acts first
**Visual:** Slide showing a stylized airline credit card with an "expires soon" tag. An arrow points to a generic chat bubble labeled "Muse".
**On-screen text:** The agent noticed first
**Source on screen:** One Useful Thing (Ethan Mollick), "The Dot and the Swarm", 2026-10-01
**Narration:** Ethan Mollick writes the newsletter One Useful Thing, and he describes a small moment. According to him, Meta's agent Muse noticed that one of his airline credits was about to expire. When he asked, it contacted American Airlines to request an extension. [01-c1] Here the software did more than answer a question. It acted on his accounts. So of everything that launched this month, what actually changes how people use their computers? And in what order does it matter?

## Scene 2: Designed around messaging you first
**Visual:** Mock phone screen with a single status line reading "Working…". A tap animation expands it into a short list of steps.
**On-screen text:** Status first, details on tap
**Source on screen:** How I AI (Claire Vo), "How Meta designed Muse… | Alex Cornell", 2026-10-12, 03:08 and 07:12
**Narration:** Muse is built around this kind of initiative. Alex Cornell, Muse's design director at Meta, said on the How I AI podcast that an agent's ability to message you first changes how you design the product. [01-c2] His example is the status line. At the top, it simply tells you the agent is working. If you tap, you see what it is working on. [01-c2] An engineer can dig into exactly what the agent did. Everyone else can stop at the first line. [01-c2]

## Scene 3: Number one, with a catch
**Visual:** Ranking slide with "1" highlighted: "Proactive agents with account access." A small key icon sits next to a mailbox labeled "spam account only."
**On-screen text:** #1: Agents that hold your keys
**Source on screen:** The Vergecast, "The AI is in the computer", 2026-10-09, 22:12
**Narration:** There is a catch. People are not handing over everything. Our read is that these agents still rank first this month, because testers already use them day to day while rationing what the agents can reach. One Vergecast host, for example, connects Muse only to a spam Gmail account. [01-c8] The new behaviour is handing an assistant standing access to your accounts. Deciding how much access to give is already part of that.

## Scene 4: Siri finds your texts
**Visual:** Side-by-side mock results. On the left, "Siri AI" with a found text message about restaurants. On the right, "ChatGPT" with "No results."
**On-screen text:** #2: OS assistants that search your stuff
**Source on screen:** Nielsen Norman Group, "The New Siri's Biggest Strength Is Not Intelligence", 2026-10-09; The Vergecast, "The AI is in the computer", 2026-10-09, 33:05
**Narration:** Second place goes to assistants built into operating systems and devices. Apple's Siri AI began rolling out as an English beta on September fourteenth. [01-c5] In Nielsen Norman Group's hands-on test, Siri AI found restaurant recommendations the author's brother had texted. ChatGPT searched the author's connected work accounts and found nothing. [01-c5] A Vergecast host reports the same strength. Siri can now search your texts and email, and it works. [01-c5] In both accounts, the part that works is plain retrieval from your own messages, not clever conversation. [01-c5]

## Scene 5: Devices that know who you are
**Visual:** Product screenshots of HomeView and a Googlebook. The HomeView screen shows a profile switcher. The Googlebook screen shows a Files search bar with a typed description.
**On-screen text:** Face switching and file search, thin app shelves
**Source on screen:** MKBHD, "Apple Homeview Impressions: Not Convinced!", 2026-10-13, 05:15; The Vergecast, "HomeView… Livestream", 2026-10-13, 28:48; MKBHD, "WTF is a Googlebook?", 2026-10-08, 03:25 and 06:47
**Narration:** Two new devices move in the same direction, toward personal context. Apple's three hundred dollar HomeView switches between up to six people by recognising their faces. [01-c6] In Marques Brownlee's impressions, it does this with a single camera and no depth or infrared sensing. It also checks that your iPhone or Watch is nearby and was recently unlocked. [01-c6] It has no voice recognition. As The Vergecast explained, anyone it does not recognise gets a limited "unrecognized user" mode. [01-c6] Google promotes Magic Pointer on its Googlebook. You wiggle the cursor and ask Gemini about anything on your screen. [01-c7] Brownlee found that Magic Pointer is off by default. His favourite feature was semantic file search, which finds a file from a description of what is inside it. He also reports that most Play Store apps are not yet built for Googlebook OS. [01-c7]

## Scene 6: Number three, the plumbing
**Visual:** Simple diagram of a box labeled "MXC sandbox" with arrows to "files" and "network," gated by a policy icon. Below it, a Gemini app toggle switches from "Chat" to "Tasks."
**On-screen text:** #3: Agent plumbing, not finished features
**Source on screen:** Windows Blog (Microsoft), "Building Windows for hybrid intelligence", 2026-10-07; Google Keyword, "Gemini 4 Argon comes to the Gemini app.", 2026-10-13
**Narration:** Third comes the plumbing for agents. On October seventh, Microsoft made its agent sandbox, called MXC, generally available on Windows 11. According to Microsoft, MXC lets organisations decide which files and networks agents can reach. [01-c4] Microsoft also says Copilot's own local context and action features will only begin rolling out on Copilot+ PCs in the coming months. [01-c4] Google says paying AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can now switch the Gemini app from chat to a tasks mode that keeps working while they are away from the computer. [01-c3] Our read is that the consumer agent apps are ahead of the platform owners. Copilot's actions are still a promise, and Siri AI is still a beta. [01-c8]

## Scene 7: What we can't say yet
**Visual:** Checklist slide with grey "unknown" marks next to: Gemini tasks tested? Copilot actions shipped? Glasses and wearables?
**On-screen text:** Thin evidence, short tests, missing voices
**Source on screen:** none
**Narration:** The evidence has limits. Much of the Muse evidence comes from Meta's own design director and from Mollick's personal account. The HomeView impressions come from about a day of use, and the Googlebook review from about a week. Nobody has independently tested Gemini's tasks mode or Copilot's local actions, and Copilot's actions have not shipped. We also found no usable evidence on smart glasses or wearables, so this ranking says nothing about them. Most of the people quoted here are tech professionals in the US.

## Scene 8: The takeaway
**Visual:** Final ranking slide: 1 Proactive agents, 2 OS and device assistants, 3 Agent plumbing. Three "watch" icons appear below.
**On-screen text:** Not better talk. Handing over keys.
**Source on screen:** none
**Narration:** The real change this month is not assistants that talk better. It is assistants that hold your keys, and people deciding which keys to hand over. Watch for three signals. First, do Copilot's local actions actually ship? Second, do independent testers put Gemini's tasks mode through real work? Third, do Muse users widen access beyond throwaway accounts? The full article and every source are linked in the description.

## YouTube description
This month's most important change in how people use computers is not better conversation. It is proactive agents like Meta's Muse acting on users' connected accounts, with users already limiting what those agents can reach. OS assistants from Apple, Google and Microsoft rank lower for now, because they are still in beta, first-generation, or promised for "the coming months."
