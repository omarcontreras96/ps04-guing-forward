# Source notes: piece 01: The agent became the front door; the hardware is still auditioning

**Argument:** The most consequential change this month is that proactive personal agents with access to your accounts went from separate experiments to the default entry point of mainstream apps. New OS agents and new devices matter mostly as places for those agents to run, and most of them are still promises rather than shipped features.

**Synthesis:** Ranked by consequence for readers: (1) Agents that hold personal context and act on it are now shipping inside the apps people already use. Google moved Spark out of a separate menu into the main Gemini chat. Mollick, a working user, reports Muse catching an expiring airline credit without being asked. (2) OS-level agents are mostly announced, not delivered. Microsoft's containment layer (MXC) is generally available, but Copilot's local context and actions are still 'coming months'. MKBHD found the Googlebook's most useful AI feature to be plain semantic file search; Magic Pointer is off by default. (3) Devices are being rebuilt around personal context: HomeView switches between users by face, and Muse is heading to glasses. (4) A privacy pushback is aimed at cameras specifically: Norway's proposed ban, a Dutch retailer halting sales, and Meta's camera-free glasses. The insight comes from combining sources. 9to5Google's report on Spark, Microsoft's Autopilot plans, UploadVR's Muse-on-glasses report and the Vergecast's argument that every new device is 'a surface' for agents together suggest the competition has shifted. Companies are less focused on the device and more on which surface hosts your agent. The regulatory and retail backlash, meanwhile, targets the camera, a visible component. The reported mishaps this month came from agents acting on account access, which none of these responses address.

**Limitations:** Meta's Muse dominates the coverage because it dominated the news; the number of sources reflects news volume, not independent corroboration. Muse's popularity rests on Mollick's statement that it topped the App Store, not on usage data. No source offers adoption or retention numbers for Muse, dots, Spark or Siri AI. Nobody has independently tested Copilot's local actions, Muse on glasses, or Guided Vision; Guided Vision is documented only by Google. Non-US perspectives are thin: Norway and the Netherlands are the only angles, and dots is not available in the UK or EEA. The ranking here is our judgment of consequence, not a measured outcome.
