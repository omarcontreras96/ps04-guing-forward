# Source notes: piece 03: Talk to delegate, look to check: what the '2001' question looks like in practice

**Argument:** HAL was the wrong model. Conversation is turning out to be the best way to hand off intent, but a poor way to check results, because a pleasant exchange feels right whether or not the work is. Readers should treat voice and chat agents as dispatchers and keep a visual or structured view for anything they will need to own.

**Synthesis:** No single source makes this argument, but the evidence lines up. Willison voice-built most of a feature while cooking, with a visual preview, then spent another half hour typing to get the details right. Plottery users relied on AI prompts most, yet the authors found prompting does not replace direct manipulation, and prompting failed for users who lacked the right vocabulary. TaskVM participants felt most in control of an agent when they could see and edit its state rather than chat with it. Alexa Plus shines at turning a spoken sentence into a routine you can then inspect in an app, and stumbles at open-ended errands. The research adds the warning. In the voice persuasion study, sycophancy was near-universal but unrelated to whether opinions changed; it shaped how users felt about the conversation. In the Copilot study, trust tracked users' confidence, not the quality of their work. Together these suggest that the qualities that make conversation appealing say little about whether the outcome is correct. That is why the products worth watching are those that pair a conversational front end with a surface for checking results.

**Limitations:** The voice persuasion study used GPT-4o, since retired, on a single topic, and measured opinions immediately after the conversation; it says nothing about long-term effects. Willison, the Plottery participants and the Alexa reviewer are all technically fluent. There is no evidence from older users, users outside tech, or blind and low-vision users, who may rely on voice far more than anyone here; Guided Vision is documented only by Google. TaskVM's trust gains come from replayed sessions. The signals listed below depend on vendor timelines that have already slipped: Meta's executives gave three different dates for Muse on glasses. If Microsoft's router or Meta's glasses agent shows reliable, self-correcting behaviour in independent tests, this argument weakens.

## 03-c7 (inference)
