# Source notes: piece 02: A confirmation step is not verification

**Argument:** Vendor descriptions of what agents can do mostly hold up in bounded, checkable tasks. Claims that agents act safely on your behalf do not: the failures independent testers report cluster where an agent acts on loose personal context, and the research shows that neither agents nor their users reliably notice when the work is wrong.

**Synthesis:** Meta says its new Muse Wonder models check in 'before following through on anything important.' Against that, the Vergecast reports two things about the Muse that has shipped: it completes purchases without a button click, and a 'yes' typed in reply to an old message started a car purchase. Kantrowitz's Muse put phone numbers under the wrong doctors' names. Part of this conflict is probably a version difference. Meta says Muse Wonder will roll out to subscribers soon, so most of these tests likely used earlier models. Combining sources, however, shows why a check-in step alone may not fix the problem. In NN/g's Siri AI test, Siri asked for and received confirmation of the starting point, then opened Maps with the wrong origin anyway. In the TaskVM study, baseline agents failed largely through premature 'done' claims. In the Copilot study, none of the authors of leaky models noticed the leakage. Claire Vo's billing-ticket test shows the other side: the same Muse model succeeded when the task had planted traps and an explicit check of the outcome. The pattern no single source states is that reliability tracks whether someone, or something, independently checks the outcome. Confirmation dialogs and an agent's own report of completion do not do that.

**Limitations:** Several consumer failures are single anecdotes: one car-purchase scare, one doctor list, one Maps route. The Meta-versus-Verge conflict may simply reflect different model versions, so it cannot be scored as a vendor claim disproven. The research consists of preprints with small or narrow samples. The Copilot study had 20 participants at one tech company on one task and was funded by Microsoft. TaskVM's user study replayed fixed agent runs, so it measures perception rather than live outcomes. Vo's benchmark is one practitioner's single run per task. We set aside the Grokbot Slack leak because it reaches us second-hand through a podcast. Users outside tech, older users and accessibility users are absent from all of this testing.
